How to Self-Publish a Book on Amazon KDP: A Step-by-Step Guide

A clear path from finished manuscript to a live Amazon KDP listing — editing, formatting, cover, and upload.

November 2026 · Publishing

TL;DR: Self-publishing on Amazon KDP (Kindle Direct Publishing) means uploading a finished manuscript and cover to Amazon's platform, which then distributes your book as an ebook, paperback, or both. Most of the real work happens before you upload anything — editing, formatting, and cover design all come first. Once your files meet KDP's requirements, publishing itself takes only a few steps.

Self-publishing a book sounds like a single action — you finish writing, you hit “publish,” your book appears on Amazon. In reality, publishing is the last step in a longer sequence that starts long before you touch the KDP dashboard. The authors who have the smoothest experience are the ones who treat manuscript preparation, formatting, and cover design as separate stages, each with its own standard to meet.

This guide walks through that sequence in order: what KDP is, what to prepare before you publish, and the specific steps to take from a finished manuscript to a live listing. It's written for first-time authors who want a clear path forward, whether you're publishing entirely on your own or bringing in help for parts of the process.

What is Amazon KDP and how does self-publishing work on it?

Amazon KDP (Kindle Direct Publishing) is Amazon's platform for authors to publish and distribute books without going through a traditional publishing house. You retain the rights to your work, set your own details like price and categories, and KDP distributes your book through Amazon's marketplace.

KDP supports two main formats: Kindle ebooks and paperback print-on-demand. Print-on-demand means a physical copy is only printed after a customer orders it, so there's no upfront printing cost or inventory to manage. You can publish in one format or both from the same account.

Self-publishing through KDP is different from working with a traditional publisher in one key way: there's no acquisitions process, no agent, and no gatekeeper deciding whether your book gets a chance. The responsibility for quality — editing, cover design, formatting — shifts entirely to you, or to whoever you choose to help you with it.

What do you need before you can publish on KDP?

A readiness checklist keeps the publishing process from becoming overwhelming partway through. Before you open a KDP account, you should have:

  • A finished, fully edited manuscript
  • A decision on which formats you'll publish (ebook, paperback, or both)
  • A formatted interior file that meets KDP's layout requirements
  • A finished cover designed for your chosen format
  • A short book description and a sense of your pricing

None of these need to be perfect on the first attempt, but each should be substantially complete before you start the KDP submission process. Trying to finalize your manuscript while simultaneously uploading files tends to create avoidable errors.

Step 1: Finish and edit your manuscript

A finished manuscript is one that has been through multiple rounds of revision, not just a first draft. Most authors underestimate how much editing a manuscript needs before it's ready for readers — developmental issues like pacing and structure, followed by line-level issues like clarity and word choice, followed by proofreading for typos and grammar.

If you're working without an outside editor, build in distance: set the manuscript aside for a few weeks before your final read-through, and read it aloud to catch awkward phrasing. If you'd rather have a second set of trained eyes on it, you can edit your manuscript before publishing with a professional editor before you move to formatting.

Step 2: Choose your formats — ebook, paperback, or both

KDP lets you publish an ebook, a paperback, or both from the same book listing. An ebook is a digital file formatted for Kindle devices and apps, while a paperback is a physical, print-on-demand copy shipped when a customer orders it.

Many first-time authors publish both, since KDP links the two formats under a single product page once both are live. If budget or time is a constraint, starting with the ebook format is usually simpler — it requires no print formatting decisions like trim size or paper color, and it gets your book on the market fastest.

Step 3: Format the interior

Formatting the interior means laying out your manuscript's pages so they meet KDP's technical requirements for your chosen format. This includes decisions like trim size (the physical dimensions of a printed page), margins, front matter (title page, copyright page, dedication), and back matter (acknowledgments, author bio).

For paperbacks, your trim size affects your margins, page count, and how your cover file needs to be sized — so it's worth settling early rather than changing it after your cover is designed. KDP offers a free previewer tool that shows how your file will look once converted, which is the most reliable way to catch formatting problems before you submit. For the specific technical requirements and current file specifications, check KDP's official help pages directly, since these details are updated periodically.

Step 4: Design a cover that fits your genre and format

A book cover does two jobs at once: it signals genre to a browsing reader, and it needs to meet the technical file specifications for your chosen format. A thriller cover and a children's picture book cover communicate very differently, and readers scanning a category page make snap judgments based on those visual cues.

Paperback covers require a different file than ebook covers, since a paperback cover wraps around a spine and back cover in addition to the front. If design isn't your strength, you can design your book cover with a professional rather than risk a cover that undersells the book inside it.

Step 5: Set up your KDP account

Setting up a KDP account requires an Amazon account, basic identity verification, payment information for receiving royalties, and tax information. This step is mostly administrative, but it needs to be completed accurately since it determines how and when you're paid.

Have your tax identification details ready before you start, since incomplete tax information can delay your ability to receive royalty payments. KDP's account setup walks you through each field, so there's little guesswork involved — just allow enough time to gather the right documents.

Step 6: Understand ISBNs

An ISBN (International Standard Book Number) is a unique identifier assigned to a specific edition of a book, used by retailers and libraries to track and catalog it. KDP offers a free ISBN for authors who don't already have one, which is sufficient for most authors publishing exclusively through Amazon.

The tradeoff is that a free KDP ISBN lists Amazon's imprint as the publisher of record, while an ISBN you purchase and own yourself lists you or your imprint instead. If you plan to distribute your book beyond Amazon — through other retailers or print distributors — owning your own ISBN gives you more flexibility. KDP's help pages outline the current rules and any costs involved, since these details can shift.

Step 7: Enter your book details

Your book details include your title, subtitle, author name, series information (if applicable), and category selections. These fields do more than describe your book — they directly affect how readers discover it through Amazon's search and browse features.

Choose categories that genuinely reflect your book's content and genre rather than the most popular or competitive ones. A well-matched category puts your book in front of readers who are actually looking for what you've written.

Step 8: Write a strong book description and choose keywords

Your book description is the sales copy that appears on your Amazon product page, and it's often the deciding factor for a reader browsing your category. A strong description opens with a hook, conveys the premise or value of the book clearly, and closes with a reason to read further — without giving away the ending.

Keywords are the search terms you select to help your book surface when readers search Amazon directly. Choose keywords that reflect how a real reader would search for a book like yours, rather than guessing at generic terms.

Step 9: Upload your manuscript and cover, then preview and fix problems

Once your files are ready, uploading them to KDP triggers an automatic conversion and preview. This is the stage where formatting issues — odd page breaks, missing images, inconsistent fonts — tend to surface, even if your file looked correct beforehand.

Review the preview carefully before moving forward, checking both the ebook and paperback previews if you're publishing both formats. Catching a formatting error here is far easier than catching it after the book is live.

Step 10: Set your price and understand royalties

Pricing your book involves balancing what readers in your genre expect to pay against what feels sustainable for you as the author. Royalties are the portion of each sale you receive, and KDP's royalty structure varies depending on factors like your chosen price and distribution options — details that are best confirmed directly on KDP's current pricing and royalty pages, since the specifics can change.

Research comparable titles in your genre to get a sense of typical pricing before settling on your own. A price that's wildly out of line with similar books can work against you either way, whether it's priced too high or suspiciously low.

Step 11: Publish and know what to expect after submission

Once you submit your book, KDP reviews it before it goes live on the marketplace. Review timelines can vary, so it's worth checking KDP's current estimates directly rather than relying on outdated figures.

After your book goes live, check your listing carefully: confirm your cover displays correctly, your description reads as intended, and your categories and keywords appear as you set them. Small errors are easy to miss in the submission process and easier to fix once you've spotted them.

What should you do after your book is published?

Publishing your book is the beginning of its life on the market, not the end of your work. A few simple, honest steps can help it find readers:

  • Ask for honest reviews. Reach out to early readers, beta readers, or your existing network and ask them to leave a genuine review once they've finished the book.
  • Build your Amazon author page. A complete author page with a bio and photo helps readers connect your name to your work, especially if you plan to publish more than one book.
  • Share the book through channels you already have. Email lists, social media, local writing communities — share the news where you already have an audience rather than starting from zero.

If you want more structured support reaching readers, Amazon book marketing services can help you build on these basics, but none of them are required to get started.

What are the most common mistakes first-time self-published authors make?

Three mistakes show up more often than any others among first-time KDP authors:

  • Skipping professional or thorough editing. A manuscript that hasn't been edited carefully is the fastest way to lose a reader in the first few pages.
  • Rushing the cover. A cover that doesn't match genre expectations can cause even a well-written book to get passed over on a category page.
  • Publishing before the interior formatting is checked in the KDP previewer. Small layout errors are easy to miss until a reader points them out.

Each of these is avoidable by slowing down at the preparation stage, which is also where most of the real publishing work happens.

When does it make sense to get professional help?

Self-publishing on KDP can be done entirely on your own, and plenty of authors do exactly that. Professional help tends to make the most sense in specific situations: when editing isn't your strength and you want a trained eye on structure and line-level clarity, when design skills are limited and your cover needs to compete visually in a crowded category, or when you'd rather spend your time writing the next book instead of managing formatting details.

If any part of the process feels like more than you want to take on alone, book publishing services can support specific stages — editing, cover design, or formatting — without taking over the whole project.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an ISBN to publish on Amazon KDP?

No. KDP provides a free ISBN for authors who don't already have one, which is sufficient if you're publishing exclusively on Amazon. You only need to purchase your own ISBN if you plan to distribute through other retailers or want your own name listed as publisher of record.

How long does it take for a book to go live after I submit it on KDP?

Amazon reviews each submission before it goes live, and the exact timeline can vary. Check KDP's current help pages for the most up-to-date estimate, since review times are periodically adjusted.

Can I publish both an ebook and a paperback version of the same book?

Yes. KDP supports publishing both formats under the same book listing, and many authors release both so readers can choose their preferred format.

What's the difference between developmental editing, copyediting, and proofreading?

Developmental editing addresses structure, pacing, and plot or argument; copyediting focuses on sentence-level clarity, grammar, and consistency; proofreading catches remaining typos and formatting errors just before publication. A thorough manuscript typically passes through all three stages in that order.

Can I change my book's price, cover, or description after it's published?

Yes. KDP allows you to update most details — including price, description, keywords, and even your cover or interior file — after your book is live, though updates go through a similar review process each time.

Is self-publishing on KDP free?

There's no upfront fee to create a KDP account or publish a book. Costs come from decisions you make along the way, such as hiring an editor, cover designer, or marketing support, and from how royalties are calculated on each sale.

Your Next Step

Self-publishing on Amazon KDP isn't complicated once you break it into its parts: prepare a finished and edited manuscript, format it correctly, design a cover that fits your genre, and work through the submission steps carefully. The quality of what you publish depends far more on the preparation stage than on the upload itself.
