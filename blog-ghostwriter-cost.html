How Much Does It Cost to Hire a Ghostwriter for a Book?

By Adams Global Publishers Editorial Team

Quick answer: Ghostwriter cost depends on three main factors: pricing model, book length, and writer experience. Most ghostwriters charge either a price per word or a flat project fee, and rates rise with research complexity, genre, and turnaround time. Expect a wide range — with experienced writers commanding more than entry-level freelancers.

You have a book idea worth telling. What you don't have is the time, or maybe the writing skill, to turn that idea into 60,000 polished words. That's the exact situation that sends most first-time authors, entrepreneurs, and executives searching for a ghostwriter — and straight into the next question: what does it actually cost?

Ghostwriter cost varies more than most new clients expect. Two writers with similar experience can quote very different numbers depending on how they structure their pricing, what your book requires, and how much hands-on collaboration you need. This guide breaks down the pricing models ghostwriters use, the factors that move the number up or down, and what should be included in a professional ghostwriting package — so you can budget with confidence and compare quotes like an informed buyer.

How Much Does a Ghostwriter Cost? A Quick Overview

Ghostwriter cost is not a single number — it's a range shaped by project scope. A short business book with minimal research might sit at the lower end of the market, while a heavily researched memoir or a technical nonfiction title with multiple expert interviews sits at the higher end.

Three variables drive most of that range: how the writer prices their work (per word or per project), how long and complex your book is, and how experienced the writer is. The next sections unpack each of these in order, starting with pricing structure, because it determines how every other factor gets calculated.

Common Ghostwriter Pricing Models

Ghostwriters typically price their work one of two ways: price per word or a flat project fee. Each model shifts risk and predictability differently between writer and client.

Price per word

The ghostwriter quotes a rate per word and multiplies it by your target word count. A 60,000-word manuscript at a given per-word rate produces a total project cost calculated directly from length.

  • Pro: Highly transparent — you know exactly how the total is calculated.
  • Pro: Easy to compare across multiple ghostwriters quoting the same word count.
  • Con: Costs can climb if the manuscript grows beyond the original estimate.
  • Con: Doesn't account for research-heavy chapters taking longer than straightforward ones.

Flat project fee

The ghostwriter quotes one total price for the entire book, regardless of exact final word count, based on an agreed scope of work.

  • Pro: Predictable budgeting — no surprises if the manuscript runs slightly long.
  • Pro: Often bundles interviews, revisions, and outlining into one number.
  • Con: Harder to compare between writers unless the scope is clearly defined upfront.
  • Con: Scope creep (added chapters, extra interviews) can trigger change orders.

Neither model is inherently cheaper. Which one serves you better depends on whether you value cost predictability (flat fee) or line-item transparency (price per word).

What Affects Ghostwriting Prices?

Ghostwriting prices move based on specific, identifiable project variables — not arbitrary negotiation. Understanding each one helps you anticipate where your quote will land before you ever request one.

  • Book length and word count. Longer manuscripts require more writing hours, so total cost scales with word count under most pricing models. A 40,000-word business book costs less to produce than a 90,000-word novel, all else being equal.
  • Genre and complexity. Fiction with multiple character arcs, or nonfiction requiring technical accuracy, demands more drafting and revision time than a straightforward how-to guide.
  • Research needs. Books requiring interviews, archival research, or subject-matter fact-checking add hours beyond the writing itself — and those hours show up in the quote.
  • Experienced vs. entry-level writer. A ghostwriter with a strong portfolio and years in the industry typically charges more than someone newer to the craft, reflecting both skill and reliability.
  • Turnaround time. Rush timelines compress a writer's schedule and often carry a premium, since the writer may need to deprioritize other projects to meet your deadline.
  • Number of revisions included. Packages with multiple revision rounds cost more upfront than those offering a single pass, but they reduce the risk of paying extra later.

Request a breakdown of how each of these factors applies to your specific project before accepting a quote.

What Is Included in a Ghostwriting Package?

A ghostwriting package defines the scope of work, not just the final word count. Knowing what's standard helps you spot gaps before signing an agreement.

Most professional packages include:

  • Interviews or discovery calls to capture your voice, story, and key content.
  • An outline that structures the book before full drafting begins, giving you a chance to approve direction early.
  • Drafting of the full manuscript in agreed sections or chapters.
  • A defined number of revision rounds to refine tone, structure, and content.
  • Confidentiality agreements (NDAs) that protect your authorship and your story.

What's often billed separately: cover design, formatting for publishing platforms, proofreading beyond the included revisions, and marketing services. Ask explicitly which of these sits inside your quote and which are add-ons. You can review sample structures in our ghostwriting packages and pricing.

Why Experience Matters (and Where to Save Smartly)

Writer experience directly affects both manuscript quality and project risk. An experienced ghostwriter has already navigated the challenges specific to your genre — pacing a memoir, structuring a business framework, building narrative tension in fiction — which reduces the number of revision cycles needed to get the manuscript right.

That doesn't mean every line item needs the premium tier. You can save smartly by separating where experience matters most (the drafting itself) from where it matters less (formatting, basic proofreading). Many authors pair professional ghostwriting services for the manuscript with separate, often more affordable, book editing services for polish — rather than paying a top-tier ghostwriting rate for every stage of production.

If you're comparing affordable ghostwriting services, ask each provider what specific experience backs their rate: genres written, project volume, and whether they offer writing samples. A lower price with no demonstrated track record is a cost signal worth questioning, not a bargain.

Red Flags When Comparing Ghostwriter Rates

Unusually low ghostwriter rates often signal a gap in experience, scope, or contractual protection — not a genuine deal. Watch for the following before committing:

  • Unrealistically low prices relative to the market for your book's length and genre. If a quote sits far below every other provider you've contacted, ask why.
  • No contract. A written agreement should define deliverables, timeline, revisions, and confidentiality terms before any work begins.
  • No writing samples. A ghostwriter should be able to show prior work (even under NDA, often via anonymized excerpts) that demonstrates range and quality.
  • Vague deliverables. "We'll write your book" is not a scope. Look for specific word counts, chapter counts, and revision terms in writing.
  • Hidden fees. Formatting, publishing support, or additional revision rounds that appear only after the contract is signed should be a deal breaker.

Review any agreement's terms carefully before signing, particularly around ownership, confidentiality, and revision limits.

How to Get an Accurate Quote

An accurate ghostwriting quote requires giving the writer enough detail to price your actual project, not a generic estimate. Follow these steps before requesting pricing:

  1. Define your target word count. Even a rough estimate (short business book vs. full-length memoir) narrows the pricing conversation significantly.
  2. Identify your genre and complexity level. Note whether your book requires technical accuracy, narrative structure, or extensive interviews.
  3. List your research needs. Flag any outside interviews, source material, or fact-checking your book will require.
  4. Set a realistic timeline. Rushed deadlines affect price, so share your target publication date upfront.
  5. Ask for a detailed scope breakdown. Request a written quote itemizing drafting, interviews, revisions, and anything billed separately.
  6. Compare at least two or three quotes. Use the same project details with each provider so the comparison is apples-to-apples.

Once you have these details ready, you can request a custom ghostwriting quote and get pricing specific to your book rather than a generic range.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is hiring a ghostwriter worth it?

Hiring a ghostwriter is worth it if you have a story or expertise worth publishing but lack the time, writing skill, or structural know-how to produce it yourself. The value comes from turning your ideas into a polished, publish-ready manuscript without the months (or years) of trial-and-error that writing alone often requires.

How long does it take to ghostwrite a book?

Ghostwriting timelines depend on book length, research needs, and revision rounds, and can range from a few weeks for a short project to several months for a longer, research-intensive book. Ask your ghostwriter for a project-specific timeline rather than relying on a general industry average.

Do I own the rights to the book?

Rights ownership should be explicitly defined in your contract before work begins. Review the agreement's ownership and confidentiality terms carefully, and ask questions about anything that isn't clearly stated.

Can I pay in installments?

Many ghostwriting providers offer installment or milestone-based payment structures tied to project phases, such as outline approval, draft delivery, and final revisions. Ask about payment structure directly when requesting your quote, since terms vary by provider.

Budget with Confidence, Not Guesswork

Ghostwriter cost reflects pricing model, project scope, and writer experience — not a fixed industry rate. Once you understand how each factor moves your quote, you can compare providers on equal footing instead of chasing the lowest number.

The most reliable next step is also the simplest: gather your project details and request a custom ghostwriting quote. A clear scope conversation upfront prevents budget surprises later and sets your book up for a smoother path to publication.
